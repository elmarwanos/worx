Experience

AR activations that actually activate

March 2026·Worx by Glimpse

At Expo 2020 we shipped a Unity-based 3D car demo for Kia and an iPad app for Hyundai's pavilion visit — both on unforgiving deadlines, both in front of audiences that don't give second chances. Here's what those projects taught us about interactive activations.

The first three seconds decide everything

On an expo floor you're competing with everything else in peripheral vision. An activation has roughly three seconds to make someone stop. That means motion, scale and an obvious "what do I do here" — instructions are a design failure.

Build for the worst network in the building

Venue Wi-Fi will betray you. Every experience we ship for events runs fully offline first; the network is for analytics and content updates, never for the core experience. This single decision has saved more launches than any other.

Make it travel

The on-site audience is the smallest audience. The real reach is the photo someone posts, the clip the brand team cuts that evening. We design every activation with its own camera moment — and pair it with online campaigns so the experience outlives the event floor.

An activation that doesn't change what people do next is just an expensive screen. Plan the follow-through before you plan the wow.
