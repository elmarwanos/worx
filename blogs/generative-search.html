Getting found in AI answers: a practical guide to generative search

Search is turning into answers. Here is how to make sure your business is one of the sources those answers are built from.

W
Written by Worx
Published September 2026
Reading time 3 min read
Share Copied
In this article Search is becoming a conversation What gets a page cited Structure beats volume Measuring what you can't click

A growing share of searches now end in an answer rather than a list of links. AI overviews, chat assistants and answer engines read the web, summarise it and cite a handful of sources. The question for any business is no longer only "do we rank?" but "are we one of the pages the answer is built from?"

01

Search is becoming a conversation

People ask longer, more specific questions when they expect an answer back: not "web design Dubai" but "how long does it take to build a bilingual website for a real estate company in Dubai?" Generative engines break that question into parts, look for pages that answer each part clearly, and stitch the result together.

That rewards a different kind of content. Pages that make a clear claim, back it up and say who is saying it are easy to quote. Pages that circle a keyword for 2,000 words without committing to anything are easy to skip.

Classic SEO asks "which page is most relevant?" Generative search asks "which sentence can I trust enough to repeat?"

02

What gets a page cited

No engine publishes its full recipe, but the patterns are consistent with everything search has rewarded for years, just more strictly applied:

  • Direct answers, early.Put the answer to the question in the first lines of the section, then explain it.
  • Specifics over adjectives.Real numbers, named processes, clear conditions. "Usually 8 to 14 weeks, depending on integrations" beats "fast turnaround".
  • Visible expertise.Who wrote it, what they do, and why they know. Author pages and a clear About page matter.
  • Consistency across the web.Your name, services and location should match on your site, your profiles and your listings.
03

Structure beats volume

Engines parse pages, so structure is how you tell them what each part means. Descriptive headings that read like questions, short paragraphs that each make one point, tables for comparisons and lists for steps all make a page easier to extract from.

Underneath that, structured data does the same job for machines. Organization, Service, Article and FAQ markup describe who you are and what a page covers in a format engines don't have to guess at. It won't rescue thin content, but it removes ambiguity from good content.

The technical basics still apply: a page that is slow, blocked from crawling or rendered only after heavy JavaScript is a page an engine may never read properly in the first place.

The short version Be the clearest answer.

Not the longest page, the most quotable one.

04

Measuring what you can't click

Answers don't always send a visit, which makes the old dashboards incomplete. Alongside traffic, track branded search volume, direct visits, enquiries that mention finding you through an assistant, and periodic manual checks of how the main assistants describe your business for the questions your customers actually ask.

Treat it as an ongoing habit rather than a project: review the questions, tighten the pages that answer them, and keep your facts consistent everywhere they appear.
